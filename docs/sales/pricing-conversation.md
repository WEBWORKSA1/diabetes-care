# Pricing conversation

Don't volunteer pricing. Wait until they ask. Most prospects will ask 50-70% into the call.

## The pricing

```
Solo (1 provider):       $399/month
Group 2-5:               $299/month per provider
Group 6+:                $249/month per provider

Add-ons (optional, per provider):
  AI Scribe:             +$99/month
  Patient Engagement:    +$149/month (portal, SMS, intake forms)
```

A solo endo on the full stack: $399 + $99 + $149 = $647/month = $7,764/year.
A 5-provider group on the full stack: 5 × ($299 + $99 + $149) = $2,735/month = $32,820/year.

## How to talk about it

When they ask "how much?":

```
Solo is $399/month. Groups get volume discounts — 2-5 providers is $299 each, 6+ is $249 each.

The AI scribe is a $99/month add-on per provider, and the patient engagement bundle is $149/month per provider — that includes the portal, SMS reminders, and intake forms.

But before we get into pricing, I want to make sure you actually want the product. Did anything I showed you today match what you were hoping for?
```

The redirect back to value is crucial. Pricing conversations that happen before value-confirmation lose.

## When they say "that's expensive"

**Bad response:** "It's worth it because..."

**Good response:** "What are you comparing it to?"

Let them tell you. The honest comparisons:

- **Athena:** $140-$300/provider/month + $599 setup + per-visit % of collections (typically 4-7%). Total cost for a solo endo running 100 visits/week at $150 average collection: $400-700/month base + $2,400-4,200/month in % = $2,800-5,000/month all-in. **Way more expensive than we are.**
- **eCW:** Similar to Athena, maybe 20% cheaper but per-visit fees still apply.
- **Practice Fusion (free):** Now ad-supported and limited. Many practices are leaving.
- **Suki / Abridge AI scribe alone:** $300-$500/provider/month. Just the scribe. No EHR.

Your pitch:
```
If you're comparing us to Athena, we're 30-50% cheaper all-in and you don't pay per-visit. If you're comparing us to a standalone AI scribe like Suki, we're 70% cheaper and you also get the EHR. The only thing we're more expensive than is doing nothing, which is what you're effectively doing if your current EHR is making you do all the work.
```

## When they ask for a discount

```
For pilot customers (first 5-10 practices), I'm offering 50% off the first 6 months — you get full access at half price while we work together to make sure the workflow fits.

After that, you're on standard pricing. No long-term contract — month-to-month.
```

Do not discount below 50%. You will set a price floor that's hard to walk back from.

## When they ask about contracts

```
Month-to-month. Cancel anytime, 30-day notice. We don't lock you in.

The one thing I'll ask is: if you're going to leave in the first 90 days, give me feedback first. I'm a one-person team and I genuinely want to know what would have made it work for you.
```

No annual contracts in Year 1. Lock-in is a privilege you earn after customers stick around.

## Trial vs paid

- **30-day free trial:** synthetic data only (uses the seed script). Full feature access. No credit card.
- **30-day free pilot (with real patients):** requires signed BAA from us, signed pilot agreement, you keep your old EHR running in parallel. We support you actively.
- **Paid month-to-month:** after pilot ends, credit card on file, standard pricing.

Most endos will want to skip the synthetic trial and go straight to the pilot if discovery went well.

## Don't do this

- Don't quote different prices to different prospects. Word travels.
- Don't bundle pricing in ways that obscure the math ("$5,000/year all-in" is opaque; "$399 + $99 + $149" is clear).
- Don't apologize for the price. It's reasonable for what's included.
- Don't compete on price alone. You'll race to the bottom.
- Don't offer to white-label or build custom features for one prospect. That's not your business model.
