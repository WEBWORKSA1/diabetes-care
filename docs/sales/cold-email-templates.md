# Cold email templates

All templates assume B2B selling to a US endocrinologist in independent practice. Keep them short. Anything over 100 words gets ignored.

## Email 1 — First touch (Subject: 6 words max)

Three subject variants to A/B (rotate weekly):

```
Subject: Built a diabetes EHR — worth 10 min?
Subject: 7,000 endos use Athena. Better?
Subject: Question about your charting workflow
```

### Body (under 90 words)

```
Dr. {LastName},

I built an EHR specifically for endocrinology practices that handles the diabetes workflow you actually have:

• Dexcom CGM data directly in the patient chart (no portal-hopping)
• AI scribe that drafts your SOAP note in 60 seconds
• SMS appointment reminders with two-way reply

Not another generic EHR. Built by 1 person, no implementation team, no 6-month contract.

Worth a 15-minute look next week?

— {Your name}
{Your title}, diabetes.care
```

### Notes
- Replace {LastName} with last name only — "Dr. Patel" not "Dr. Anika Patel".
- Do NOT say "revolutionary" or "AI-powered" — every healthcare email says that.
- Mention Dexcom by name. It's the highest-signal word an endo will see in the body.
- Sign with a real name. "Founder" is fine if you have no MD.

## Email 2 — Follow-up after 4 days (no reply)

```
Subject: Re: {original subject}

Dr. {LastName},

Looping back in case the original got buried.

If charting time isn't a pain point for you, I'll stop emailing — just reply "not now" and I'll close the loop.

If it is, here's what 15 minutes looks like: I show you a real charting workflow against your most common patient type (T2DM follow-up). You tell me if it would save you time. No pitch.

— {Your name}
```

## Email 3 — Break-up email after another 7 days (still no reply)

```
Subject: Closing the loop

Dr. {LastName},

I'll stop following up. If anything changes — EHR contract renewal, frustration with charting, looking at AI scribes — my email is below.

One last thing: I wrote up how the AI scribe handles a 12-minute diabetes follow-up: {Link to one specific blog post or screenshot}. Might be useful even if we never talk again.

— {Your name}
```

Break-up emails get 20–30% reply rates if the first two didn't. People feel slightly guilty.

## Email 4 — Warm intro (from a mutual connection)

When someone introduces you to an endo:

```
Subject: Intro from {MutualName}

Hi Dr. {LastName},

Thanks {MutualName} for the intro. {MutualName} mentioned you've been frustrated with {their EHR}, particularly around {whatever the mutual told you}.

I built an endocrinology-specific EHR — happy to show you the parts most relevant to your practice. 15 minutes, no slides.

Here's my Calendly: {link}
Or reply with 3 times that work for you.

— {Your name}
```

Warm intros convert at 25–40%. Treat them as the highest-priority queue.

## Tracking

Keep a spreadsheet or Notion DB with columns:
- Name, Practice, Email, NPI
- Source (LinkedIn / NPI / warm)
- Email 1 sent date, opened, replied
- Email 2 sent date, opened, replied
- Email 3 sent date
- Call scheduled / completed
- Status (cold / replied / call / trial / customer / lost)
- Notes

Reply rates < 3% means your subject lines or list are wrong. Reply rates > 15% but no calls means your CTA is weak.
